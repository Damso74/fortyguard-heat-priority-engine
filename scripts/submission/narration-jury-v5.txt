Phoenix has 4,288 transit stops. In extreme heat, a small field team cannot inspect them all.

A heat map tells you where it is hot. It does not tell you where to act first, or whether that choice will survive scrutiny.

Heat Priority Engine does.

It starts with 450 real FortyGuard temperature cells across 27 Downtown Phoenix stops. Time, units, footprint, and source stay visible. If the evidence cannot prove a persistent hotspot, the product says so.

Then the planner stress-tests every candidate. At capacity ten, three stops stay selected across all 324 scenarios. Seven move when assumptions change. That is the difference between a shortlist and a defensible priority.

One click turns a stop into a field mission. The inspector records shade, shelter, accessibility, and confidence. Nothing changes until a human reviewer accepts the evidence.

The result is a new plan, plus an audit package that preserves the run, the assumptions, and the decision trail.

Not another heat map. A defensible way to decide where the field team goes next.
