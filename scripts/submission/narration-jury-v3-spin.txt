Phoenix has four thousand two hundred eighty-eight active transit stops. When extreme heat arrives, a small field team cannot inspect them all.

A heat map can show where it is hot. It does not answer the operational question: where should we send people first, and can we defend that choice?

Without a repeatable process, scarce capacity can go to priorities that change with assumptions. Field observations remain disconnected, and leaders receive a recommendation instead of an audit trail.

That is why I built Heat Priority Engine. It turns FortyGuard temperature intelligence into a reviewable field decision.

This Downtown pilot begins with real evidence: four hundred fifty stored temperature cells from three completed FortyGuard activities, captured at eight a.m., two p.m., and eight p.m. across twenty-seven stops. The monitor preserves the footprint, source time, units, and evidence digest. When an evening anomaly does not persist, the product refuses to call it a hotspot. Trust starts with knowing what the data does not prove.

The planner opens on the verified default instead of asking the operator to configure a model. At a field capacity of ten, three stops remain selected across all three hundred twenty-four scenarios. Seven depend on assumptions. One useful change, increasing team capacity, shows how the queue responds without hiding the original result.

Then a ranked stop becomes a field mission tied to the source run. An inspector records shade, shelter, accessibility, confidence, and notes. The observation cannot silently rewrite the thermal ranking. A human reviewer must accept it before the plan advances to version two.

The scenario lab compares the plan with simple alternatives on the same eligible stops. If a simple baseline performs better, the product says so.

Finally, the audit report packages the decision, assumptions, review state, run identity, and evidence digest for leadership and downstream systems.

Heat Priority Engine gives transit teams a practical answer to three questions: where should we look, why this place, and can we prove how the decision was made?

The value is not more heat data. It is a defensible way to act on it.
