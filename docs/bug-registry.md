# Bug registry

Findings from the smoke test of the pre-pivot interface (2026-08-04), plus
defects found afterwards in the shipped engine (#9-#24). The old weight-slider
journey was replaced rather than repaired, so this registry records what was
found, whether it survives into the new journey, and what was done about it.

Every engine defect here changed reported numbers. The Central Phoenix weekday
demo run splits **13 robust / 37 assumption-dependent** after the hardening pass.
The count is unchanged, which is a coincidence and not a reassurance: the day-type
fix moved it to 12 / 38 and the Pareto tie fix moved it back. Every figure here
was re-measured after the last fix, not carried forward. Saturday and Sunday now
split differently from the weekday — they could not before, because weekend runs
borrowed the weekday timetable — and the current figures are carried in one place
only, [`scoring-methodology.md` §1c](scoring-methodology.md): **17 + 33** on
Saturday and **20 + 30** on Sunday, same area and capacity. Quote them from there
rather than from here, so a re-measurement has one file to change. All of these
are **synthetic fixture outputs**; see
[`findings-provenance.md`](findings-provenance.md).

Status: `FIXED` · `OBSOLETE` (the surface it affected no longer exists) ·
`CARRIED` (still live in the new journey, must be fixed) · `OPEN`.

| 9 | **High** | Expected wait charged the **whole** scheduled gap to any hour the gap merely overlapped. `Σgap²/(2Σgap)` is only correct when the gaps tile the analysed hour; a gap crossing a clock-hour boundary was weighted by its full length and normalised by the total gap span rather than by the hour. Wrong in both directions: one 160-minute gap (departures 09:00 and 11:40) reported **80 minutes for both** 09:00–10:00 and 10:00–11:00, where the true values are **130** and **70**. In a metric that multiplies wait by an hourly temperature, this moved load to the wrong time of day. | `tests/unit/exposure.test.ts` → `RULE 6b` | `FIXED` — each gap is clipped to the arrival window before it is weighted, and the wait is integrated over the window. The closed form is recovered exactly when the gaps tile the hour. Six regression tests, including one asserting the clipped sub-intervals tile the hour exactly for five timetables × four hours. |
| 10 | **Medium** | A wait cap was applied as `min(E[W], c)` — the expectation computed first, then clipped — while the stated justification for the cap ("nobody waits eleven hours; riders time their arrival") is a claim about **each rider's own wait**, i.e. `E[min(W, c)]`. The two differ: for one departure at 14:30 over the 14:00 hour, `min(E[W],15) = 15.00` but `E[min(W,15)] = 13.13`. The old form also pinned many stops at exactly the cap, an artefact readable as a real tie. | `tests/unit/exposure.test.ts` → `RULE 7b` | `FIXED` — the cap is now defined as per-passenger truncation inside the integral, documented in `lib/metrics/waiting.ts`, `docs/scoring-methodology.md` §1.2.2 and on the methodology page, and exported as `waitCapRule` on every run. Seven regression tests, including cap monotonicity and `E[min(W,c)] ≤ c`. |
| 11 | **High** | Weekend ridership was paired with the WEEKDAY timetable: the GTFS extraction produced one profile and `dayCategory` selected only the ridership column. Saturday runs 5,476 trips against a weekday's 7,854, so the wait term was understated by 30-40% on exactly the days service is thinnest. | `tests/unit/hardening.test.ts` -> day types | `FIXED` - three day types extracted separately; the dataset carries no shared timetable to borrow. ## Not bugs, but recorded

- **Heat and ridership are statistically independent** across Central Phoenix
  on the pre-pivot diagnostic. That measurement is no longer reproducible from
  this tree and has been removed rather than quoted; what stands without it is
  that nothing converts degrees into riders, which is reason enough to report two
  axes rather than blend them.
- The 380 px sidebar map was a design decision, not a defect. It was the wrong
  decision for a geospatial product, and the map is now the primary surface.
